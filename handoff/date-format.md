# Dates: short day and month

12 November 2026 · Kate Hester

Every full date ExecHQ shows is now written short: **Thu, Nov 12, 2026**, not Thursday, November 12, 2026. This replaces the spelled-out style set on 2 November. Nothing else about dates changes.

## The rule

| Part | Written | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day of the week | First three letters, then a comma | Thu, |
| Month | First three letters, no full stop | Nov |
| Day | Number, no leading zero, then a comma | 12, |
| Year | Four digits | 2026 |

- Three letters for every month and day, with no exceptions (Sep, not Sept; Tue, not Tues).
- A range joins two dates with a spaced en dash: **Sun, Oct 18, 2026 – Sat, Oct 24, 2026**.
- A time follows after a comma: **Mon, Oct 5, 2026, 9:14**.
- Relative words stay as they were: "today", "yesterday", "on Tuesday" (this week), "last Tuesday". Those name a day in full, because they are speech, not a date.
- Screen readers: the Admin Dashboard's calendar column headers still carry the full day name for assistive technology. Nothing else needs a separate spoken form.

## Where it shows

Everywhere a full date appears: version history, the Library, the Briefing's dateline, Signals and presence entries, the plan's check-ins and direction card, Profile, LinkedIn dates and the Admin Dashboard's date range. In the prototype they all come from one formatter, so production should keep one too.
