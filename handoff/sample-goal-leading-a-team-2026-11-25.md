# The sample person's goal: leading a team

25 November 2026 · Kate Hester

From Eric's Playbook review, decided with Eric and the team on 25 November (option C of "The sample person's goal"). Maya's goal, the stand-in every screen is built around, changes everywhere at once. A real user writes her own; only the sample changes.

| | Her goal |
| --- | --- |
| Before | I want to move from running campaigns to leading a broader marketing organization. |
| Now | I want to move from running campaigns to leading a team. |

Eric wrote "running social campaigns" on one screen and "running campaigns" on the others; "running campaigns" is used, to match her title, Senior Director, Campaigns.

## What changes with it

- **Her short forms:** "leading a team" after "Toward" and in Playbook drafts; "lead a team" in Home's weekly question, "How ready do you feel to lead a team?"
- **Onboarding's sample answer**, typed by the mic, and the story in her Playbook library.
- **The Plan's reasoning** for a direction like hers: "You said you want to lead a team, so this plan starts with the narrative and the evidence that the people deciding will ask for." The read-back and the skipped-refinement line say "lead a team" the same way.
- **Briefing:** each read's "Why it's here" and the publishers' "Chosen for leading a team."
- Her plan is unchanged: still Step Up (Increase leadership scope).

## Left as they are

- The plan's own words: the "A broader remit" signal, "Map who decides on a broader role", and "You've asked for the broader role" are Step Up's language for any person on it, not quotes of her goal.
- The onboarding option "Lead a bigger organization" and its lines, which belong to that option.
- Dated briefs that quote the old goal.
