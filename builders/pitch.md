# Pitch Builder

**Makes: a sendable pitch for speaking, a podcast or press, with its follow-up.** Marked used as **Sent**.

**Status: draft.** From the *Sprint 4 Experience Brief* and the *Functional Scope* (3.2 Pitch Builder), and what the prototype does today. Lines marked **Proposed** are Claude's, for Kate to confirm. Rules every builder shares are in `builders/shared.md`; this brief has the same seven sections as the other three.

## 1. Builder card

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **What it makes** | A pitch draft; two or three subject lines; an expanded version; a one-line angle and why now; a follow-up for when she hears nothing. |
| **What it is for** | Getting her onto a stage, a show or into a publication that the people above her pay attention to. |
| **When to use it** | When there is a stage, a show or an outlet worth pitching. |
| **Use case screen** | "A pitch to a conference, a podcast or an editor, with subject lines and the follow-up already drafted." / "Use it when there is a stage or a show the people above you pay attention to." |

**One pitch, one history.** The follow-up is part of the same artifact, not a separate one, so the Loop shows one pitch with one history.

### 1.1 How people arrive here

- **From an action step** whose tool is Pitch Builder:

| Plan | Action step |
| --- | --- |
| Step Up | 2.1 Choose one relevant visibility channel and create a credible proof-of-value artifact or activity (when the channel is a stage or show) |
| Make Your Mark | 3.2 Send a tailored pitch to one event, podcast or publication on her list |
| Make the Move | 3.1 Prepare the ninety-second version and say it out loud |
| Chart a New Course | 2.2 Talk to people already doing the work in each direction |

- **In the prototype's mock plan:** "Pitch yourself to a podcast the people above you follow" (arrives with Podcast chosen), "Draft a talk proposal for the leadership forum" (Speaking, the leadership forum), and "Put yourself forward for the Q1 planning review" (Speaking, the Q1 planning review).
- **From "What's on your mind?"** when what she says names a pitch, podcast, conference, panel, speaking, "a talk", a keynote, press, an editor, a journalist or a call for proposals.
- **From the Toolbox or the library,** and from her last pitch ("Your last pitch. Start from it instead of a blank page."), which starts a new pitch linked back to it.

### 1.2 When another builder fits better

**Proposed.**

- The invitation already came, and she is preparing for the talk → **Situation Brief**.
- They asked for a bio, not a pitch → **Positioning Builder**.
- She wants to show her point of view first, so a pitch has something to point to → **Thought Leadership Builder**.

## 3. The draft

"Here is a first draft of your pitch, with its follow-up. Change anything; it is yours."

| Section | What it is for | Her words | ExecHQ's wording | Left as a question when missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Subject lines** | Three to choose from | Her angle (the first line) | "A [session / conversation / story] idea for [who]"; "From someone doing the work, not watching it" | What would you talk about? |
| **Pitch** | The email itself, short | Her angle, her proof | "Hi …, I'd like to suggest a [session / conversation / story] for [who] on … It matters now because … I can send a short outline." | Who are you writing to? Why does this matter to their audience this month? |
| **Expanded version** | When they want more | Her links | "The three points I'd cover: … I can work to your format and length." | What are the three points you would cover? |
| **Angle and why now** | The one line to keep in her head | Her angle | "Why now: …" | What makes this timely? |
| **Follow-up, if you hear nothing in a week** | Sent later, from the same artifact | Her angle | "Hi, following up on my note about … Happy to fit your schedule, or to suggest someone else on my team if the timing is wrong." | None |

Rules for this builder:

- **The word matches the kind:** a session for speaking, a conversation for a podcast, a story for press.
- **Her angle fits the sentence.** As the first subject line it keeps its capital; mid-sentence ("…a conversation on how planning…") it doesn't.
- **Never invents the person.** "Hi" is followed by a question, never a guessed name.

## 5. Using it

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Copy** | "Copy to send" |
| **Export** | None. A pitch is pasted into an email, not attached. |
| **Mark ready, then sent** | "Mark ready", then "Mark sent", which asks two things, both optional: Sent to (filled with who she pitched) and When (today, or an earlier day). Sending is always hers; ExecHQ never sends. |
| **Check back** | 5 days after the day she sent it (**Proposed**) |
| **The question** | "You sent your pitch for the Q1 planning review on Thursday. What came of it?" "Nothing yet" is the common answer for a pitch, and asks again later. |
| **Signal Picture** | Once sent, where she sent it and what came of it, under the plan area it serves, tagged with where it went |
| **Reuse** | "Use as a start" makes a new pitch from this one, with its own Loop record, linked back ("Started from …") |

## 6. How the agent behaves here

**Proposed, all of this section.** It adds to the rules every builder shares (`builders/shared.md`, section 8).

- **Short.** A pitch someone reads in ten seconds. The expanded version is where detail goes.
- **Their audience, not hers.** Why now is about the outlet's audience this month, not her career.
- **Proof she gave, nothing more.** It never inflates a title, a team size or a result.
- **One pitch at a time.** It never suggests sending the same pitch to several outlets at once.
- **"Nothing yet" is expected.** It never treats an unanswered pitch as a failure, and suggests the follow-up only after a week.

## 7. Open questions

1. **Short and expanded versions.** The brief asks for "short and expanded versions where relevant". The prototype's pitch is the short one, plus an expanded version. Is that enough, or does each need its own section?
2. **Check-back days.** 5 days after sending is proposed. The follow-up section says "if you hear nothing in a week". Should the check-back match, at 7?
3. **Topical angle and audience.** The brief asks for both. The prototype asks the angle and takes the audience from her direction without asking. Is the direction enough?
4. **The ninety-second version** (Make the Move 3.1) is something she says, not sends. Is Pitch Builder the right tool for it, or the Situation Brief?
5. **Talking to people in a new direction** (Chart a New Course 2.2) is an outreach note, not a pitch. Is Pitch Builder the right tool, or does it need its own type?
6. **Her positioning statement in the pitch.** "Goes with this" says the pitch draws on it for who she is, but the draft doesn't use it yet. Add a line from her short bio, or take the promise off?
