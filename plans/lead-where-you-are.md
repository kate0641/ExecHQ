# Lead Where You Are

**Template: Strengthen influence and presence in your current organization.** User-facing name: **Lead Where You Are**.

**Status: draft.** The name, template and stages are Kate's (12 October 2026). **Everything else is Proposed**: drafted by Claude from Step Up's recipe and the prototype, for Kate to confirm. Rules every plan shares are in `plans/shared.md`.

## 1. Plan card

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Intended user situation** | A leader whose work is solid but under-seen inside her own organization. She wants more influence and presence where she is, without necessarily asking for a new title yet. |
| **Desired outcome** | The people whose view matters know what she does and why it matters, hear it in the terms decisions are made in, and come to her; and she is framing the internal opportunities she wants. |
| **Common constraints** | Limited time, internal politics, a manager who may not advocate for her, a matrixed organization where credit is shared, and discomfort with self-promotion. |

### 1.1 How people arrive here

- Refinement answer: "My ability to influence where I am" under *What are the areas you're seeking to build or enhance?*
- Direction answer: none of Kate's examples routes here yet. A proposed one: "I want senior leadership to know what my team delivers."

**When another plan fits better**

- She wants to ask for a broader role now → **Step Up**.
- The audience she cares about is her field, not her company → **Make Your Mark**.
- A specific internal conversation is set within about 30 days → **Make the Move**.

### 1.2 Stages and success signals

| Stage | Success signal |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Map the stakeholders** | She has named the people whose view of her work matters most, what each cares about, and how each hears about her work now. |
| **2. Sharpen strategic communication** | Her role and a recent win are written in her leadership's language, and her regular updates use it. |
| **3. Create visible moments** | She has led or presented at least one moment the right people saw, and more people outside her team know what she does. |
| **4. Frame internal opportunities** | She has named a problem that should come to her by default, framed it as an opportunity, raised it with the right person, and recorded the answer. |

## 5. How the agent behaves on Lead Where You Are

It adds to the rules every plan shares (`plans/shared.md`, section 5).

- **Their language, her work.** It translates her work into the terms her leadership uses, never into jargon of its own.
- **Generous with credit.** In a matrixed organization, visibility that takes credit from her team backfires. It helps her show impact while naming who she did it with.
- **Careful with politics.** Her stakeholder map stays private. It never suggests going around her manager, and helps her bring a manager along rather than past them.
- **Small, regular moments.** It favours steady visibility (updates, 1:1s, one session) over a single big push.
- **No promise of a role.** It helps her be seen and trusted; it never says that leads to promotion.
