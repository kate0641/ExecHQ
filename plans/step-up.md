# Step Up

**Template: Increase leadership scope.** User-facing name: **Step Up**.

**Status: draft.** From *ExecHQ: Content and Mapping* (Kate, 12 October 2026). The source calls its action steps "proposed examples, not finalized coaching guidance", and so does this brief. Lines marked **Proposed** are Claude's, for Kate to confirm. Rules every plan shares are in `plans/shared.md`.

## 1. Plan card

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Intended user situation** | A strong individual contributor or manager seeking promotion to a broader leadership role. |
| **Desired outcome** | A credible case for expanded scope, and a clear leadership narrative she uses in promotion and stakeholder conversations. |
| **Common constraints** | Limited time, sensitive internal politics, and incomplete external visibility. |

### 1.1 How people arrive here

- Direction answer: "I want to lead a larger marketing organization."
- Refinement answer: "My case for broader scope or promotion" under *What are the areas you're seeking to build or enhance?*

**Proposed: when another plan fits better.**

- A specific promotion or scope conversation is already set within about 30 days → **Make the Move**, which prepares for one moment.
- What she wants is to be known in her field more than a bigger role → **Make Your Mark**.
- She wants more weight where she is, without asking for a bigger role yet → **Lead Where You Are**.
- She is not sure the broader role is what she wants → **Chart a New Course**.

### 1.2 Stages and success signals

Success signals are qualitative and observable: things she has done or has, never a score.

| Stage | Success signal |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Clarify leadership proposition** | She has a clear articulation of the scope she wants, a credible leadership proposition, a stated assessment of her current context, and an accepted initial roadmap. |
| **2. Build and make visible the evidence for broader scope** | She has accumulated and recorded evidence of broader-scope work, strengthened her visibility in the channels that matter, and completed plan actions that show readiness. |
| **3. Prepare for and advance the scope conversation** | She has prepared for or held consequential conversations, recorded the outcomes and feedback, and has a clear next decision, commitment or adjustment to the plan. |

## 2. Action playbook

Each stage's action steps, with the conditions that make each one appropriate. The agent recommends the step whose trigger matches her situation now. A trigger can bring a later stage's step forward: a scope conversation that is already scheduled calls for 3.1 straight away.

The **Tool** and **Follow-up** lines are **Proposed**: the source does not name them.

### Stage 1: Clarify leadership proposition

#### 1.1 Write a one-sentence statement of the scope she wants to lead and why she is ready

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Horizon** | Short-term |
| **Trigger** | She chose broader scope or promotion as her main need, or she can't yet say what scope she wants next. |
| **Done when** | She saves an editable statement and confirms it reflects the scope she wants to pursue. |
| **Evidence ExecHQ keeps** | The saved direction statement, its edit history, her confirmation. |
| **Plan area and signal** | Plan area: leadership proposition. Signals: published content and LinkedIn are prepared as future expressions of the proposition. |
| **Tool** | Positioning Builder. In the prototype, onboarding's one-sentence positioning statement is the first version. |

#### 1.2 Add three examples of work that show enterprise-level judgment, cross-functional influence or broader leadership potential

| | |
| --- | --- |

| **Trigger** | Her direction is clear, but her evidence is too general to support a credible narrative. |

### Stage 2: Build and make visible the evidence for broader scope

#### 2.1 Choose one relevant visibility channel and create a credible proof-of-value artifact or activity

For example: publish a targeted piece, submit a speaking proposal, or send a tailored pitch.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Horizon** | Medium-term (30 to 60 days) |
| **Trigger** | She has a defined proposition and initial examples, but little visible proof in the channel that matters most. |

| **Evidence ExecHQ keeps** | The artifact's lifecycle state, the channel she reports, an optional outcome update, the draft-to-final artifact versions. |

### Stage 3: Prepare for and advance the scope conversation

#### 3.1 Prepare for a named promotion, performance, staffing or scope conversation

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Horizon** | Short-term |

| | |
| --- | --- |

## 4. How the agent behaves on Step Up

**Proposed, all of this section.** It adds to the rules every plan shares (`plans/shared.md`, section 5).

- **The case, not the campaign.** Every step serves one thing: a credible case for broader scope. Visibility work is recommended because it becomes evidence, never for its own sake.
- **Careful with politics.** Internal politics are a named constraint. It never suggests going around her manager or surprising the person who decides. It helps her choose who hears what, and when.
- **Outcomes over activity.** It asks for the result, the stakeholders and her role in each example, so her evidence reads as enterprise-level judgment rather than a list of tasks.
- **The conversation decides the pace.** A scope conversation on the calendar takes priority over everything else in the plan.
- **No promise of the role.** It helps her make the case well; it never says the case will win.
