# Find your next direction

**Template: Explore and clarify a next direction.** User-facing name: **Find your next direction**.

**Rewritten for a move within the company (Kate, 18 November 2026).** ExecEngine doesn't steer anyone out of their company, so this is the plan for working out a move *inside* it: another function, or another part of the business. It replaces the brief written for leaving, called Chart a New Course (12 October 2026). The name, the template, the three stages, their finish lines and the first step are the product as built in onboarding (Onboarding spec, Appendix C). **Everything else is Proposed**: drafted by Claude from the old brief and the prototype, for Kate to confirm. Rules every plan shares are in `plans/shared.md`.

---

## 1. Plan card

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Intended user situation** | A senior leader who wants a different role inside her company, in another function or another part of the business, or who feels stuck or restless where she is and can't yet name what's next. |
| **Desired outcome** | A direction inside the company specific enough to plan around, chosen on evidence rather than nerves, with a story that makes the move look deliberate, someone in the company helping her get it, and a hand-off to the plan that gets her there. |
| **Common constraints** | Discretion: her manager and team may not know she is looking yet. Limited time. An identity tied to her current role. Not knowing how moves between areas happen in her company. A fear of choosing wrong. |

### 1.1 How people arrive here

As built in onboarding (Onboarding spec, 3.4b to 3.7):

- **She picks it** on Where do you want to start?, from "You want a different role here, or aren't sure yet which." Its signs: "You feel stuck or restless, but can't name what's next." · "You're curious about another part of the business." · "You want to choose on evidence, not nerves."
- **ExecEngine picks it** after I can't decide, when her direction is **Make a move within my company** (narrowed to "Move into another function, like marketing to sales" or "Move to another part of the business") or **I'm not sure yet**.
- **The question before the plan** is "What's made you think about a move?" The answer **I've hit a ceiling in my area** sends her to Step Up instead: a ceiling in her area is a scope problem.
- **The questions that make it hers** are "Which part of the business are you curious about?" and "Who have you talked to about it?". Their answers name the first areas she tests (2.1) and who she already knows (2.2).

**When another plan fits better**

- She can name the role and has a moment coming, an internal interview or a conversation about the move → **Make the Move**.
- What is wrong is the size of her role, not its direction → **Step Up**.
- She wants more say where she is, not a different role → **Lead Where You Are**.
- She wants to be known in a new area before moving into it → **Make Your Mark**.

**Never a move out.** If she says she wants to leave the company, the agent says that is outside what ExecEngine helps with and offers the closest plan inside it (`plans/shared.md`, 2.3). Nothing in this plan prepares a search outside: no recruiters, no outside applications, no CV.

This plan is meant to end. Its last step hands her to one of the other four.

### 1.2 Stages and success signals

Three stages, as onboarding, Home and the Plan page show them. The Plan page adds no stage to this plan.

| Stage | Done when (shown to her) | What she will have |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Work out what travels** | You can name the strengths you'd take anywhere in the business. | What she is good at, separated from where she has done it; the parts of the job she wouldn't miss |
| **2. Test it cheaply** | You've seen the work up close, and crossed off what doesn't fit. | Four conversations with people in adjacent roles; two directions ruled out on evidence, not nerves |
| **3. Choose your direction** | You can name the role you want, and someone is helping you get it. | A direction specific enough to plan around; a story that makes the move look deliberate |

## 2. Action set

## 5. How the agent behaves on Find your next direction

It adds to the rules every plan shares (`plans/shared.md`, section 5).

- **Inside the company, always.** It helps her move to another function or part of the business. It never suggests leaving, applying elsewhere or talking to recruiters.
- **Curious, not prescriptive.** It asks before it suggests, and never tells her what she should want.
- **Evidence over nerves.** It helps her rule options in or out for real reasons, and notices when fear is doing the deciding.
- **Discreet.** Her manager and team may not know she is looking. It never suggests anything that would make her exploring visible at work until she chooses to, and 3.3 lets her decide when her manager hears.
- **Low risk first.** It favours small, reversible tests over asking for the move until she has a direction.
- **It aims to end.** The plan succeeds when she leaves it for another plan, not when she stays.

## 6. Open questions

None. The five raised by the first brief are settled by the rewrite (Proposed, with the rest):

1. **"Generate a new spark"** is folded into 2.3, See the work up close: a small, real taste of the work, inside the company.
2. **The plan ends with a hand-off** (3.4). Early steps toward the new role belong to the plan she moves to.
3. **The order of the stages** follows onboarding's three. 2.3 can come before every conversation in 2.2 is done.
4. **Her lists, directions and conversations** are kept as her answers on each step, on the Plan page. They need no screen of their own in V1.
5. **Pace** is the prototype's four weeks a stage, a suggested pace, never a deadline.
