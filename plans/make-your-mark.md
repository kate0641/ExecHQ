# Make Your Mark

**Template: Enhance overall executive presence.** User-facing name: **Make Your Mark**.

**Status: draft.** The name, template, stages and the routing example are Kate's (12 October 2026). **Everything else is Proposed**: drafted by Claude from Step Up's recipe and the prototype, for Kate to confirm. Rules every plan shares are in `plans/shared.md`.

---

## 1. Plan card

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Intended user situation** | A senior leader whose work is respected inside her organization, but who is not yet known in her field for a point of view of her own. |
| **Desired outcome** | A clear point of view, said in her own words, consistently in writing and in front of external audiences, kept up at a rhythm she can sustain. |
| **Common constraints** | Limited time, her employer's rules on speaking publicly, discomfort with self-promotion, and uncertainty about what is worth saying. |

### 1.1 How people arrive here

- Direction answer: "I want to be more visible as a leader in my field."
- Refinement answer: "My visibility and point of view" under *What are the areas you're seeking to build or enhance?*
- Driver: "My desire to stand out in my field as an influential thought leader."

**When another plan fits better**

- The visibility is a means to a promotion or a bigger role → **Step Up**.
- One talk, panel or appearance is coming up and is the whole concern → **Make the Move**.
- The audience she cares about is inside her company → **Lead Where You Are**.

### 1.2 Stages and success signals

| Stage | Success signal |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Define your point of view** | She can say what she stands for in one sentence without naming her employer, and has a short list of topics she is willing to defend. |
| **2. Put it in writing** | She has published under her own name on at least one of her topics, and her LinkedIn profile says the same thing her writing does. |
| **3. Take it to external audiences** | She has pitched, and ideally been accepted by, at least one event, podcast or publication that her audience follows, and recorded how it went. |
| **4. Sustain a cadence** | She has a rhythm of writing and outreach that fits her week, has kept it for a stretch, and knows which channels are working. |

## 2. Action playbook

The agent recommends the step whose trigger matches her situation now. An accepted invitation brings 3.3 forward at any point.

### Stage 1: Define your point of view

#### 1.1 Write how she describes herself, in her words, not her employer's

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Horizon** | Short-term |
| **Trigger** | She chose visibility or point of view as her main need, or she describes herself only through her job title and company. |
| **Done when** | She saves a short self-description and confirms it sounds like her. |
| **Evidence ExecHQ keeps** | The saved statement, its edit history, her confirmation. |
| **Plan area and signal** | Plan area: point of view. Signals: the base for LinkedIn, published content and every pitch. |
| **Tool** | Positioning Builder. Onboarding's one-sentence statement is the first version. |
| **Follow-up** | None. |

#### 1.2 Name three to five topics she is willing to defend, with her stance on each

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Horizon** | Short-term |
| **Trigger** | She has a self-description but no clear subjects to speak or write on, or her topics are too broad to say anything distinctive. |
| **Done when** | She records at least three topics, each with a one-line stance someone could disagree with. |
| **Evidence ExecHQ keeps** | The topic list and stances, a completion timestamp. |
| **Plan area and signal** | Plan area: point of view. Signals: creates source material for published content, speaking, podcasts and press. |
| **Tool** | Positioning Builder, adding her topics. |
| **Follow-up** | None. |

## 3. Signal emphasis

Internal only, never shown to her. A dash means the plan does not recommend work in that signal in that stage.

| Signal | 1. Define your point of view | 2. Put it in writing | 3. Take it to external audiences | 4. Sustain a cadence | How it adds to executive presence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **LinkedIn** | Foundation: settle the self-description her profile will use | Primary: align the profile and post her writing there | Evidence: share appearances and coverage | Primary: keep a steady, recognizable presence | The first place people check what she stands for |
| **Press** | Foundation: identify topics where she has a credible angle | — | Primary: pitch selectively where her point of view fits | Evidence: record and reuse coverage | Third-party credibility for her point of view |
| **Speaking** | Foundation: identify topics she could speak on | — | Primary: pitch for and prepare talks and panels | Evidence: carry audience response into the next pitch | Shows authority in the room, not only on the page |
| **Podcasts** | Foundation: identify points of view suited to an interview | — | Primary: pitch for and prepare appearances | Evidence: use appearances to earn the next invitation | Depth and reach for her point of view |
| **Published content** | Foundation: turn her topics into stances | Primary: publish under her own name | Evidence: point pitches to what she has published | Primary: keep publishing at her rhythm | Durable proof of how she thinks |
| **AI discoverability** | Primary: make her public description coherent and accurate | Evidence: check her writing makes her findable on her topics | Evidence: check appearances reinforce the same picture | Primary: keep her footprint coherent as it grows | Shows whether her field can find her for her point of view |

## 4. How the agent behaves on Make Your Mark

It adds to the rules every plan shares (`plans/shared.md`, section 5).

- **Her voice, not her employer's.** It helps her say what she thinks, and steers away from restating company messaging.
- **A stance, not a summary.** It pushes for a point someone could disagree with, because that is what gets remembered and invited.
- **Respect her employer's rules.** It reminds her to check her company's policy before she speaks for the first time on a sensitive subject, and never suggests saying anything confidential.
- **Fewer, better channels.** It recommends one or two channels that fit her audience and time, never all six at once.
- **Invitations are earned, not promised.** It helps her pitch well; it never says a pitch will be accepted.

## 5. Open questions

1. **Routing.** Is "My desire to stand out in my field as an influential thought leader" always a route here, or only alongside the direction answer?
2. **Stage 3 and Step Up.** Both plans pitch and prepare for appearances. Is the difference (presence as the goal here, presence as evidence in Step Up) clear enough for routing?
3. **Pace.** No suggested period per stage yet. The prototype uses four weeks a stage.
4. **Employer policy.** Should onboarding ask whether she needs approval to speak publicly?
5. **The outlet list (3.1) and the body of work (4.3).** Neither has a V1 tool. Are they V1?
